### W2 follow-up — the motor interlocks declared as they actually are, and §3.1.6(d) resolved

`INTERLOCK.DEFEAT` scoring had no join key: `DRV-M202` was not a `raiseTrip` source and no M202 interlock existed as declarable logic, so "scored from the declared matrix" had nothing to look up. Anthony's ruling of 2026-09-13 resolved it by **making the honest reading standard rather than bespoke**.

**Every cause row now carries the same four fields — `latch`, `reset`, `lockout`, `permissive` — whatever family it belongs to.** A field that does not apply is `null`, never absent, and a test fails on a *missing* field rather than a null one. Ten rows: seven process trips (six at the `onTrip` seam), two motor trips, one permissive.

A fifth field, `enforced`, carries the distinction that makes the motor rows worth having. A process trip really shuts the valve. The P-101 level permissive really refuses the START. **A motor trip latch does neither** — `motorCmd` sets `m.trip=false` and runs the motor, then stamps the defeat. The plant *permits and records*. Declaring that is true of the code and teaches the real control philosophy; declaring an inhibit that does not exist would have been a fiction with a console display's authority behind it.

**The lockout is 30 s, not 15.** `tripMotor` sets `m.lock=30` after a trip; `motorCmd`'s STOP path sets `15` after an operator stop, and the app's own Point Detail says both: *"30 s after trip, 15 s after stop"*. An earlier draft of the contract had 15, from reading the STOP path alone — caught by applying Anthony's own rule ("every row true of the code, or no row") to the instruction before the code.

**The join key is the effect column**, `DRV-P101` / `DRV-M202`, named exactly as `motorCmd` builds it (`'DRV-' + tag`). A string equality, and a test pins it against the app's own template so the join cannot break silently and leave every annotation quietly unresolvable.

**"Scored from the matrix" means annotation, and that boundary is proved, not promised.** `annotateDefeat(target, causeState)` resolves the defeat to its column, names the cause, states that the latch is advisory and the reset is a recorded manual START after the lockout, names the enforced permissive where one exists, and reports the cause-state at reset. It returns exactly `{resolved, target, column, causes, text}` — a test asserts that shape and the absence of any `score`/`cap`/`penalty`/`severity`/`weight` key, and asserts that `src/cause-effect.js` never reaches the drill scorer **in code**. `src/drill-arch.js` is untouched; **no A-drill score moves**. Spec §3.1.6(d) reworded to match; spec at rev 4.

### Logged, not fixed: drill A5 penalises a restart after the cause has cleared

`docs/dev/A5-RESTART-SCORING-DEFECT.md`. Anthony asked for this to be verified and logged **outside** W2, and it is: no scoring code changed.

A5's gate fires on **every** accepted M-202 restart following a trip, regardless of whether the cause still holds — because **nothing anywhere consults cause-state**. `motorCmd` reads `wasTrip = !!m.trip`, which records only that the motor was tripped at some point; the `ActionEvent` shape carries no fault status, so even a scorer that wanted to distinguish has nothing to read; and `P.faults` has zero matches in `src/drill-arch.js`. Worse for A5 specifically, the `agit` upset is a one-shot that clears itself in the same call that trips the motor, so **the cause has always already cleared by the time a restart is possible** — the penalised case is the only case.

Measured: a textbook-correct A5 run scores **100 / pass**; the identical run plus a restart 40 s after the cause cleared and the lockout expired scores **75 / fail**. Confirmed by two independent probes, two independent skeptics each reproducing it from scratch, and the architect's own run.

Two things worth carrying past the defect itself. A5's `gateDescription` says the lesson is *controller-domain staleness*, but the mechanism tests "was this motor ever tripped" — **intent and mechanism are about different things**. And the existing test covers this gate by hand-setting `m.trip=true; m.lock=0`, never driving the real fault-injection path — so it proves the **wiring** and never asks whether the **conditions of firing** are right. A test that pins a mechanism is not a test that the mechanism is correctly conditioned.

### W1 — the declared plant map and specification integrity at drill start

Work item W1 of `docs/dev/CONVERGENCE-SPEC.md` (item 6), built to `docs/dev/W1-BOUNDARY-DOF-CONTRACT.md`. **No golden moved**; `stepU1`..`stepU4` untouched.

**Item 6 was renamed after it was built** (Anthony, 2026-09-13), from "explicit boundary streams and a degrees-of-freedom check" to **"specification integrity at drill start"**. The old title promised a DOF check over boundary streams; the build found that check cannot fail on this plant, and a name that writes a cheque the plant does not cash is worse than an awkward one. `docs/dev/CONVERGENCE-SPEC.md` goes to **rev 3** with the rename, the two-lane reframing, §3.4.6(b) moved to a lane B test, and §4.1.4 binding item 1 to *read* the plant map.

**What W1 actually found, which is not what it set out to build.** Item 6 was specified before anyone had read the plant. A six-agent read-only mapping pass plus a completeness critic established two things at `dfee660`, and the second one reshaped the work:

- **The plant is three islands, not a train.** Exactly three fields of `P` cross a unit boundary, all U3 → U4: `P.h.f` (flow), `P.h.pre` and `P.h.bed` (temperatures), written in `firedHeater`/`fixedBed` (`src/models.js:549,565,591`) and read in `separator` (`:648,654`). Unit 01 has **zero** `P`-field coupling to Unit 03 — U3's feed comes from `V.FV310.pos` on its own `FIC310` loop, not from any U1 output — and Unit 02 is fully standalone. Declaring the islands is as load-bearing as declaring the boundary: it is what stops item 1 inventing couplings that do not exist.
- **The DOF check as specified cannot fail on this plant.** Over-specification needs a second actuator contending for a boundary variable; U4 owns none upstream of its own reads. Under-specification needs a variable that can float; an explicit time-stepped model with exactly one owning write per tick has none. No preset or drill `basePreset` ever overrides a controller *mode* — they set `sp` only.

**And the control-loop lane turned out to be nearly as well-posed.** The lane was built on the reading that a cascade master in AUTO with its slave out of CAS is two specifications for one final element. `tests/app-instructor.test.js` falsified that within minutes of the wiring landing: it does `setMode('TIC202','MAN')` and then starts D1, and the refusal blocked it. The test is right — taking a slave to MAN while its master tracks is ordinary practice and is drill D6's whole premise (`src/models.js:52`). The jacket has exactly one specification, the operator's OP; the master is idle, back-calculating through INITMAN (`src/pid.js:106-128`) so the return to CAS is bumpless. `CASCADE_OPEN` was demoted from `refuse` to `note`.

So the finding of W1 is not the check. **The finding is that this plant is well-posed by construction** — one boundary with no possible contention, and a PID layer that makes every mode combination well-defined, since `transferMode` already refuses CAS without a master. What ships refuses only the two states that are genuinely ill-posed and reachable by *corruption* rather than by operation, and reports everything else.

### Known limitations
Carried forward from v2, recorded but not yet addressed by any landed v3 stage:
- `c.step(dt)` draws a fixed number of seeded PRNG samples regardless of `dt`, so a trajectory depends on the *number* of step calls, not on simulated time -- a replay that reconstructs elapsed time with a different `dt` will diverge. Debrief replay uses the same step sequence as the live run, so it is not hit when replay is driven from the journal.
- A12's `BIASED_MEASUREMENT` targets `XMTR-TIC201`, which is not one of the three reserved legacy pairs, so the fault is engine-health only and does not drive `src/models.js`. The cascade the drill describes is not a process cascade in this wiring; ACK is unreachable and the R-201 trip abort does not fire. Same honest 60-point ceiling as the other engine-only A-drills.
- Engine-only A-drills (A2, A4-A11, A12) cannot earn the stabilize (ACK) category because they raise no process alarm. The debrief category is now reachable from ARCH Debrief YES/NO while the A-drill is still active; clean-run fixtures still drive Diagnose only, so their committed scores are unchanged.
- A12's INTERLOCK.DEFEAT gate still targets `XMTR-TIC201`. Motor START only synthesizes `DRV-<tag>`. The A12 gate is not live. A5 (`DRV-M202`) is.
- `restoreSnapshot()`'s event truncation is boundary-inclusive (`e.t <= t`); restoring the same snapshot instant twice in one process accumulates a duplicate bookkeeping event.
- `ESS.Kpi.scoreDrill`'s other-trips row computes `-Math.min(cap, 0)`, a negative zero, when a drill has no other-equipment trips.
- Two `Math.random()` fallbacks and one `Date.now()` fallback remain in `src/models.js` and the app for when `ctx.rand` is absent. The app always seeds, so they are unreached in practice; the new determinism guard (above) exists so a future caller that forgets `ctx.rand` fails loudly instead of silently reaching one.

### Known limitations
- Drill D4: cutting reactor feed and leaving it cut fills TK-101 within about 9 minutes; the guidance now says to restore feed, and the tank trip is scored as a secondary deduction rather than the drill's trip.
- Sub-priority is a per-condition default (by condition kind); it is not editable from the station.
- The DAS map is a fixed six-trigger table. M202 TRIP suppresses TIC213 PVHI only; FIC211 PVLO is not listed as a follow-on because it is phase-managed (active only in FEED).
- KPI windows (10/30/60 min) are measured on real elapsed time, so a session younger than the window reports over a shorter span (the window text says when the session started); the standing-alarm threshold is 10 min at the training scale, not the EEMUA field value.
- Loops with a derivative term (TIC201, TIC301, TIC311, TIC212) jitter several per cent per step on measurement noise in every mode; judge bumpless transfer on a P+I loop.
- The tube-skin trip re-opens FV311 when it resets, so TIC311 left in MAN at a high output cycles the trip (same behaviour as the bed trip).
- Initial conditions run the process forward synchronously (the batch presets take up to about 5000 steps), so the page pauses briefly while a preset loads.
- `endDrill` does not clear the `drift` upset, the TI216 shed latch or the skin trip (they are not drill faults); RESET PROCESS or a preset clears them.
- A snapshot restore truncates the journal to the snapshot time, so the E-SIGNATURE event of a signed restore is dropped; the CONFIG entry written afterwards carries the name and reason.
- The message list can exceed 400 entries by the number of pending CONFIRM messages.
- ENGR trip-point edits on the phase-managed U2 points last only until the next phase change.
- In hidden mode the DRILL IN PROGRESS banner remains visible (it names no fault); only the drill-armed and drill-ended messages are withheld.
- The coverage matrix is built from our own 42-task inventory, not from vendor course or video titles; the 80 % pass mark is this simulator's own threshold and is independent of any vendor certification.
- Alarm Help, the philosophy page and all rationalisation fields are our own prose and describe this simulator only; they are not site alarm documentation.
- The standalone reuses the React runtime blobs already in `dist/`; `tools/build-dist.py` requires an existing dist to rebuild from.
